QSHIELD · Public security model · v1.4.0

Threat model

QSHIELD provides evidence about quantum-software integrity. It checks files and workflows for known suspicious patterns; it does not prove that a circuit, compiler, provider, or result is safe.

Software-only scope. No QPU, device SDK, or hardware validation is part of this release.

Assets and trust boundaries

  • Circuit integrity: source, gate structure, parameters, and expected behavior.
  • Supply-chain integrity: dependency declarations, installed package metadata, and analysis configuration.
  • Analysis environment: the QSHIELD process, its dependencies, local files, and CI configuration.
  • External services: quantum providers, package registries, and optional AI services are outside QSHIELD's control.

With the local Workbench, the browser sends circuit input to the Workbench server. The optional AI features are separate cloud operations; AI triage is designed to send finding summaries rather than raw circuit source. Do not treat a client-side scan or policy result as provider-side enforcement.

Threats in scope

T1 · Gate tamperingInserted, substituted, or perturbed gates may alter a circuit. QSHIELD can flag known structural patterns and compare circuits with supported exact or formal methods. It cannot detect every semantically harmful modification.
T2 · Information leakageAncilla use, measurement changes, or suspicious structure can expose information. Available checks cover selected known cases; they are not a general side-channel detector or a guarantee of confidentiality.
T3 · Supply-chain compromiseDependency auditing can identify selected risky pins, typosquatting indicators, and known advisories. A clean audit does not establish package authenticity. Full signed provenance and attestation workflows are not yet implemented.
T4 · IP exposureQSHIELD can provide local analysis and integrity evidence, but it cannot stop a provider, compromised host, or other recipient from copying or misusing submitted intellectual property.

Assumptions and mitigations

  • Inputs are untrusted. Parsers apply size, complexity, and time limits; malformed or out-of-scope inputs should fail explicitly.
  • Findings are evidence for human review. An empty finding list means only that no implemented check matched.
  • Results may be inconclusive when a method, optional dependency, or resource limit prevents a check.
  • Keep the unauthenticated Workbench on loopback. Non-loopback binds require --allow-remote; remote deployments need network restrictions and an authenticated TLS proxy.
  • Keep the core analysis local and offline where possible. Enable cloud AI only when its data handling is appropriate for the circuit and findings.

Out of scope and limits

  • Proving a circuit or backend honest, detecting all adaptive attacks, or guaranteeing that a clean scan is safe.
  • Protecting a compromised operating system, developer workstation, CI secret, or quantum provider.
  • General side-channel defense, real-hardware validation, and claims about field detection rates.
  • Signed circuit provenance, a control plane, and enterprise high-availability gate operations; these remain planned work.

Metrics currently reported by QSHIELD are fixture-corpus measurements, not field rates. For vulnerability reporting, see SECURITY.md. Return to the Workbench.
